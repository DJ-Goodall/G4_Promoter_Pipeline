Diagnostic on the first end-to-end run of branch `2026-08-10_V1.1` (HEAD `5cf966f`,
"Apply the 2026-08-07 audit corrections to the pipeline").

Two questions are answered here:

1. **Did every rule execute, and does v1.1 close the points raised in the
   [2026-08-07 audit](audit-2026-08/20260807_Audit_of_Pipeline.md)?**
   Yes for every acceptance criterion, and the headline scientific result survives the
   correction. Four defects were found, all now fixed — one a rule that had never executed

   comes down to exactly one file. [§6](#6-provenance-local-overlay-vs-a-from-scratch-run)
   gives a ~1 h check that settles it.

`docs/audit-2026-08/` remains the record of *what was changed and why*. This is the record

## 4. The re-derived result — it survives

The audit's central consequence was that removing the heuristic back-fill invalidates the
reported `hybrid > parallel > antiparallel` TSS ordering until it is re-derived. Measured on
this run, that ordering holds — in both arms and in all four genotypes.

Peak mean BG4 signal at the TSS (`tss_topology_profiles.csv`, `…_argmax.csv`):

| genotype | arm | hybrid | mixed | parallel | antiparallel |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| WT | primary (`precision: 0.80`) | **12.87** | 10.05 | 9.18 | 6.02 |
| WT | sensitivity (argmax) | **12.18** | — | 9.13 | 6.11 |
| DHX36KO | primary | **19.00** | 15.34 | 14.09 | 10.20 |
| DHX36KO | sensitivity | **18.09** | — | 14.06 | 10.29 |
| FANCJKO | primary | **14.56** | 11.68 | 10.44 | 6.85 |
| dKO | primary | **20.33** | 16.61 | 15.21 | 11.54 |

The two arms bracket the abstention policy: the primary arm withholds G4SP's low-confidence
calls, the sensitivity arm forces every one of them by argmax. The ordering is stable across
that entire range, so it is a property of the data rather than of the threshold.

**Where the abstentions live.** 13,570 of 60,508 PQS-bearing union peaks (22.4 %) are
`mixed`. Forcing them by argmax grows hybrid 1,010 → 5,578 and antiparallel 1,322 → 1,475,
which is why the primary arm is the honest default: four out of five peaks in a forced
hybrid stratum would be peaks the classifier declined to call.

**The audit's estimate was right.** Applying v1.0's back-fill rule to this run's
abstentions, the pqsfinder heuristic would have supplied **84.8 %** of the hybrid stratum
(5,655 back-filled vs 1,010 genuine) and **72.8 %** of the antiparallel stratum (3,546 vs
1,322). The audit predicted 87.0 % and 66.1 % from the v1.0 tables. Since the heuristic
scores 2/10 on literature controls, those strata were largely a measurement of the
back-fill, exactly as the audit argued.

**The two methods still disagree at chance level.** κ = −0.0092 over the 46,938 peaks where
both give a definite call, with marginals too different for κ to be readable (G4SP 95.0 %
parallel, heuristic 35.9 %). The marginal-free version: of the heuristic's 18,438 hybrid
calls, 391 (2.1 %) are also G4SP-hybrid; of its 11,650 antiparallel calls, 38 (0.3 %) agree.
`mixed` propagates correctly into every downstream stratified table checked
(`topology_by_feature.csv`, `rloop_signal_by_topology.csv`, `tss_topology_profiles.csv`,
`topology_composition_compare.csv`).

### D4 — `verify/run_all.sh` reported success unconditionally

The verification suite printed **ALL v1.1 VERIFICATION CHECKS PASSED** while three of its
five checks were failing. Two independent causes:

**The harness could not detect failure.** Each check ran as
`if "$RSCRIPT" "$2" 2>&1 | grep -v "..."; then :; fi` followed by a test on
`${PIPESTATUS[0]}`. The `:` builtin executes *after* the pipeline and resets `PIPESTATUS`,
so the subsequent test always read 0. Every check was reported green regardless of outcome —
including the syntax pass, which is the one part that had been genuinely informative.
**Fixed:** the pipeline now runs unguarded and `rc=${PIPESTATUS[0]}` is captured immediately.

**Check 1 asserts v1.0 numbers against whatever is in `results/tables/`.**
`rederive_audit_numbers.R` re-derives every REVIEW.md claim from the v1.0 tables and
`stop()`s on mismatch — which is correct until the v1.1 pipeline overwrites those tables,
after which it fails at the first assertion by design (`n where both methods definite`
46,938 vs the claimed 101,638). **Fixed:** the script now reads from `$V11_TABLES`
(defaulting to `results/tables`), and `run_all.sh` materialises the v1.0 tables from
`3c1b264` via `git archive` into a temp dir — which writes nothing to the index or the
working tree. Check 1 is now pinned to the baseline it was written against, which is what
"so the review cannot drift from the data" requires.

**Confirmed after both fixes:** all five checks pass, exit code 0, zero `[FAIL]` lines,
`git diff --cached` empty. Check 1 re-derives all 43 v1.0 assertions including κ = −0.0098,
the 87.0 % / 66.1 % back-fill shares, ERCC 114,980 > WT 107,276, 5′ UTR fold 11.904, and
G4SP 8/10 vs heuristic 2/10.

> **Environment note.** Run `bash verify/run_all.sh` with the system R on `PATH`, *not*
> inside `conda activate G4` — the conda environment shadows R 4.5.1 with an R that has no
> Bioconductor packages, and checks 3–5 die with `no package called 'GenomicRanges'`. Until
> D4 was fixed that failure was invisible.

### One more measured correction

Beyond renumbering: the README's "the peak caller has no null"
limitation said the ERCC controls yield *more* peaks than the real samples. With
`z_per_replicate: true` and `min_width: 1000` that ordering inverts (ERCC BG4 26,686 vs WT
44,737) — the v1.1 peak-calling change did what it was meant to do. The limitation stands,
because a control that merely scores lower is still not a null, and the README now says

## 6. Provenance: local overlay vs a from-scratch run

### The overlay cannot change a result

`config/config.local.yaml` overrides **six keys, all of them paths**:
`paths.bigwig_dir`, `paths.g4sp_repo`, `rnaseq.count_table`, `expression.count_table`,
`lncrna.gtf_local`, `unclassified.rmsk_local`. No threshold, window size, contrast, seed or
model parameter is touched — Snakemake merges the overlay recursively into
`config/config.yaml` and everything else is inherited. So the overlay changes the answer
only if the files it points at differ from the files a from-scratch run would download.
Those are the 28 GEO bigWigs, the GSE269081 count table, GENCODE vM25 and mm10 rmsk — the
same public artefacts `scripts/download_data.*` fetch. The bigWigs are checkable against the
`bytes` column of `data/geo_manifest.tsv`; see the recipe below.

### The real risk was carry-over on this working tree

`cache/peaks.rds` was rewritten at 22:04 with the new `z_per_replicate` / `min_width: 1000`
parameters. Every cached artefact was classified by whether it predates that point:

**Correctly invalidated.** Everything peak-derived encodes the peak count in its cache key —
`peak_pqs_n62913.rds`, `motif_catalog_n62913.rds`, `feature_profiles_n62913_*`,
`sequences_scored.rds` — and all of it was rebuilt after 22:04. This is the mechanism
working as intended.

**Legitimately reused.** `cache/g4rl_sig_*` (18:28–18:35) and
`cache/cooc_prof_*` / `cooc_ko_diff_*` (18:54–19:29) predate the new peak set, and rules 39
and 40 re-ran at 21:47 and 22:22 without rebuilding them. Reading the code shows this is
safe: those caches are built over `prom` / `prom_prof` / `win`, all derived from
`cache/regions_promoter.rds` (TxDb promoters), **not** from the peak set — the one
peak-dependent step in script 40, the G4+/G4− promoter stratification, is not cached.
Worth naming anyway as a latent hazard: unlike the `_n62913` family, these cache keys encode
no config value at all, so a future change to `promoter_half_width`, `cnt_norm` or the
genotype list would silently reuse them.

**Genuinely stale — one file.** `results/tables/g4sp_control_benchmark.csv` is dated
**2026-07-30**, before the branch existed. Rule `control_benchmark` (26b) was skipped
because its output existed and its declared inputs had not changed. It is the input to the
new v1.1 comparison table `topology_control_benchmark_compare.csv`. Script 26b was *not*
modified by the v1.1 commit, so the content is expected to be identical — but that is an
inference, not a measurement. Snakemake agrees, and says so unprompted: a dry run reports
*"1 jobs have missing provenance/metadata … Rules with missing metadata: control_benchmark"* —
this is the only rule in the workflow whose output it cannot account for.

### The check that settles it

A full clean-room run is ~5 h and unnecessary. Only three artefact families have unproven
provenance and each can be forced individually — no pqsfinder sweep, roughly 1 h.

```bash
conda activate G4
BWDIR="C:/Users/Danie/Documents/GitHub/Hubrecht_postdoc/Bioinformatics/Sato2025_analysis/RNA-seq/data"

# 1. Are the local bigWigs byte-identical to what a from-scratch download would fetch?
awk 'NR>1 {print $3, $2}' data/geo_manifest.tsv | while read -r want name; do
  got=$(stat -c%s "$BWDIR/$name" 2>/dev/null || echo missing)
  [ "$got" = "$want" ] || echo "MISMATCH $name want=$want got=$got"
done

# 2. Force the one stale input plus the two rules with config-blind caches.
rm -f cache/g4rl_sig_* cache/cooc_prof_* cache/cooc_ko_diff_*
snakemake --cores 4 --configfile config/config.local.yaml \
  --forcerun control_benchmark g4_rloop_promoter_correlation g4_rloop_cooccurrence

# 3. If nothing moved, the tree is provenance-clean.
git diff --stat results/tables/
```

Note the `--` separator if a target is passed after `--configfile`; the flag is variadic and
will otherwise swallow the target.

If step 1 reports no mismatch and step 3 shows no change to
`g4sp_control_benchmark.csv`, `g4_rloop_promoter_*.csv` or `g4_rloop_*.csv`, this run is
equivalent to a from-scratch v1.1 run and can be reported as one. Anything that does move is
a genuine carry-over and belongs in the record.

### A note on reproducibility for anyone else running this

A clean clone has no `cache/`, so none of the above applies to a third party: they get a
from-scratch run by construction. The hazard is specific to a working tree that has carried
results across a parameter change — which is exactly what happened here, and why the check
is worth running once rather than assumed.

## 7. Limitations carried forward

These are properties of the analysis, not defects in the run. All are stated in the README's
Limitations section, now with measured values.

- **DeepG4 AUROC 0.591** against GC-matched background is close to the 0.5 null.
  `background_gc_matching.csv` rules out a truncated control set, which leaves two live
  readings: GC matching removes the signal (GC drives both DeepG4 score and BG4 occupancy),
  or a substantial fraction of called peaks are not G4 sites. `22_sanity_check.R` now warns
  on this automatically. The DeepG4 panels are diagnostic, not validation.
- **Topology remains unresolved for 22.4 % of PQS-bearing peaks.** Reporting them as `mixed`
  is honest, not a fix. G4SP's own 8/10 on literature controls is the ceiling here.
- **κ is not interpretable between these two methods** given 95.0 % vs 35.9 % parallel
  marginals; the marginal-free rates are the number to quote.
- **20 of 60 `p_binom` rows underflow to exactly 0**, and peaks are not independent
  Bernoulli draws — they cluster with mappability, GC and accessibility, which the binomial
  cannot express. Read folds, not p-values.
- **No IgG or input track exists in the GEO submission**, so the peak caller has no null;
  ERCC is a batch control with the same BG4 antibody, not a specificity control.
- **No ATAC track in the manifest**, so the G4 × R-loop promoter correlation has no
  accessibility covariate.
- **No real permutation test.** `regioneR::permTest` with `circularRandomizeRegions` hangs
  on Windows; the width-aware binomial is the substitute, and is now named as such
  everywhere.
- **Magnitude claims inherit someone else's spike-in scaling** from Sato et al.; this
  pipeline has no independent check on it.
